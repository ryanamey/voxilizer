# Reading notes: Complete Vocal Technique (Sadolin, 2012 edition)

Paraphrased notes taken while reading the full book (OCR of the founder's PDF), for Voxilizer design work. Page numbers refer to the PDF. Rules here are CVT pedagogy, not settled science; treat them as guidance. Where these notes disagree with `updates-since-2012.md`, the updates win.

## Intro / method (p7-19)

- Philosophy: separate taste from technique; all sounds valid; singer chooses. Technique must work instantly; if it hurts / feels wrong it IS wrong.
- Practice: fail an exercise 3x in a row → make it easier (risk of uncontrolled constriction). Easy w/o mistakes > hard w/ mistakes. Practise close to real situation. Exercises simple, one technical problem at a time; then transfer to song ("return to a simple exercise when a song problem appears"). Transpose exercises through keys. Personalised training programme.
- 4 subjects (the "CVT in 4 pages" flow chart p19): 3 overall principles → choose MODE → choose SOUND COLOUR (light/dark) → perhaps choose EFFECT.
- Exact vowels matter: 11 CVT vowels w/ IPA: EE[i] see, I[ɪ] sit, EH[e] stay, A[æ] and, OO[u] you, O[ʊ] woman, OH[ou] so, AH[ɑ] far, OE[ɜː] herb, UH[ʌ] hungry, OR[ɔː] order.
- 2012 update: in Curbing (high) only I, O, UH. Overdrive (high) only EH, OH. Edge (high) only I, EH, A, OE. Low part: modes resemble each other, all vowels seem ok.
- "You can't feel a mode, you hear it."
- Neutral for women above C6 only (? "Neutral is obtainable for women only above High C" — likely means full-volume / note context; check later).
- Volume: 4 main volumes: loud, medium loud, medium quiet, quiet.
- Modes summary:
  - Neutral: non-metallic; with air / without air; all vowels, all colours; pp–mf; ff only Neutral w/o air in high part. Found via loose jaw. Only mode where breathy is healthy.
  - Curbing: half-metallic, "hold", moan/whine; mp–mf only; high part vowels I/O/UH; colour alterable quite a lot.
  - Overdrive: full-metallic, "bite", shout "hey"; upper limit women D5/Eb5, men C5; no lower limit; high part only EH/OH; mostly loud.
  - Edge: full-metallic, twang/duck, scream/diving airplane; all parts of voice; only twanged vowels (I, A, EH, OE) high; colour only light; loud.
- Sound colour: vocal tract — epiglottic funnel, larynx, tongue, mouth shape, soft palate, nasal passage. Train individually, then combine. Control principles + mode before colour.
- Effects list: distortion, creak/creaking, rattle, growl, grunt, screams, intentional breaks, air added, vibrato, ornamentation. Effects must be designed per singer (anatomy, fitness, energy, temperament). Control principles, mode, colour BEFORE effects.

## Overall principles (p20-)

- Support, Necessary twang, Avoid protruding jaw & tightening lips. Must be obeyed regardless of mode/colour/effect.
- Support values: relative 0–100 effort; depends on pitch (high AND low need more), volume (multiplicative), vowel, colour, time since inhalation, mode. Very quiet (pp) needs disproportionate support. Long notes: support rises over time; descending after a high peak keeps peak support.
- Natural vs active support; plus side/zero point/minus side. Don't take too much air. Forcing = breathy but loud (too much airflow) → damage.
- Energy: more problems from too LITTLE energy than too much. Rough/raw/powerful singing needs even more strength.
- Uncontrolled constriction = main enemy (not constriction itself).
- Larynx rises on high notes, lowers on low; colour tweak within that.

## p49-70

- Open throat = image; really = avoid uncontrolled constriction via support + necessary twang + "centre of the mode".
- Jaw: lower jaw backward/down; finger test. Lips may tighten in low part; not in high.
- Twang: epiglottic funnel narrowing (arytenoids → petiole). Necessary twang (+10–15 dB, clear, non-breathy; often inaudible as twang) vs DISTINCT twang (snarl/cackle, lighter colour). Distinct twang works with EE, I, EH, OE, A; problematic with OH, O, OO, AH → shift (OH→OEH, O→OE, OO→OOE, AH→A). Twang ≠ nasality.
- Vowels: tongue placement (sides on upper molars for EE→I→EH→A progressively further back; OE like I + rounded lips; OO/O/OH/AH tongue tip low, moving back). "Merged sound": vowels converge in high part — don't fight it.
- Diphthongs: two vowel parts may imply two modes (e.g. "sane": EH=Overdrive, EE=Neutral) → breaks. Choose which part to sustain.
- Consonants: tighten briefly; release immediately on vowel. Consonants may need extra support.
- Mouth opening by pitch: women C4–A4 / men C3–A3 lips may tighten; A4–E5 / A3–E4 drop jaw; above E5/E4 drop jaw + sideways smile; low G3–C4 / G2–C3 drop jaw; very low below G3/G2 drop + smile.
- Completion: end of note needs MORE support & twang (don't slacken).
- Attacks = beginning of a note in a mode: Overdrive/Edge attack (glottal), Curbing attack, Neutral w/o air attack, Neutral with air (H). All healthy if done right. Attack mode ≠ sustained mode. Reach support value BEFORE the note.
- Volume 1–10: Neutral 1–4, Curbing 4–7, Overdrive & Edge 7–10. Never louder than your support allows. Extremes of a mode need more support. Pianissimo & thinning only in Neutral; need lots of support + twang. Crescendo/decrescendo: change mode if needed; decrescendo needs MORE support/twang.
- Registers rejected → "parts of the voice" by pitch: women very low <C3, low <C4, middle C4–C5, high C5–C6, very high >C6 (flageolet). Men: very low <C2, low <C3, middle C3–C4, high >C4 (to C5), very high >C5. Breaks come from mode changes, not registers.
- Very low part: vocal cords may only produce sound every other vibration → octave lower (period doubling!). Trained with distinct twang/Edge as "drill".

## p70-101

- Voice types (soprano..bass) irrelevant for popular singers; men speak/sing 5th–6th lower than women. Vocal cord length women 12–17mm, men 17–24.
- Old-person vibrato = insufficient support.
- Fine-tuning: poor pitch is caused by uncontrolled constriction, not bad hearing. Don't fixate on tuning; record and look for constriction. Practising high/low intonation deliberately.
- Unintentional breaks = mode change (vowel/volume/pitch "lures" to another mode, or failing to support), OR flageolet below high C while getting louder. Not registers. Know when you're near a mode's limit and choose to change mode before the voice does.
- Support × volume multiply: e.g. 1,2,4,8,16 at vol 5 → 5,10,20,40,80 at vol 10. High (and low) notes at high volume get enormous. When a singer runs out, voice drops into lower-energy mode.
- "If the same exercise fails three times in a row it's too difficult."
- Descending lines: voice wants to fall into a heavier mode (Overdrive) — needs more support to stay in Neutral/Curbing.
- Split in voice (flageolet below C6) = "unintentional creaking". Treat by singing loud.
- MODES CHAPTER INTRO: 95% of technical problems from incorrect use of modes. CENTRE OF THE MODE (healthiest) + MARGINAL AREA (variation still within limits) + outside → danger. Marginal area: big in Neutral, small in Edge; shrinks with pitch; further out = more support & risk. Modes as gears. Air ONLY in Neutral; attempting air in other modes → severe constriction. Metal scale: Neutral 0, Curbing 1/2, Overdrive/Edge 1. Transition may need an "assisting mode" (p133). Pitch "pulls" toward easier mode; maintaining a mode not favoured by pitch needs extra support. Support ≠ volume. Psychological barriers to loud modes.
- Each mode chapter structure: overview, conditions, finding, images, sound, pitch, vowels, volume, sound colours, classical, exercises, warnings, unintentional distortions.
- NEUTRAL: non-metallic. With air (max mp) / without air (pp–ff; ff only in high part: women ≥C5, men ≥C4). All pitches, all vowels (EE, OO, AH easiest), all colours (infinite). Loose jaw. Dark colour: necessary twang, lowered larynx, compressed tongue, relaxed mouth corners, raised palate, closed nasal. Light: distinct twang, raised larynx, broad tongue, smile, lowered palate, open nasal. Classical = twang + lowered larynx. Powerful twanged non-metallic tone sounds shrill/distorted via mic. METAL-LIKE NEUTRAL (Curbing-like, Overdrive-like, Edge-like): Neutral w/o air, twanged, lighter colour, borrows vowels/pitch rules of target mode BUT must stay mp–pp; used in R&B/pop/MT. Warnings: don't sing powerfully in Neutral w/o air in MIDDLE part of voice (strain). UNINTENTIONAL DISTORTION in Neutral = scratching/jarring = metallic while positioned for Neutral (bite, hold with too little volume, too loud, trying to remove air with constriction, jaw/lips/support) → like car hitting the rumble strip. Want distortion? practise it separately.
- CURBING: half-metallic, "hold", restrained/plaintive (moan, stomach ache, whimper). Never add air. All pitches (no lower limit but weak/squeezed low; easiest middle). Women use F4–F5 when powerful but not Edge-aggressive. Vowels: I, O, UH (high). EE/OO tend → Neutral; EH/OH tend → Overdrive (replace EH→I, OH→UH initially). A→UH. Back vowels lower larynx → raise larynx. Volume mp–f (no pp, no ff); higher pitch → louder needed. Too loud → full metal while hold kept → strain. Quiet passages: letting go of support in Curbing → sore. Below mp → switch to Neutral. Colour: alterable a lot but darker = risk losing metal. Raise palate with pitch carefully.

## p102-122

- Curbing colour: darker = necessary twang, carefully lower larynx (the lighter a metallic mode, the safer), raise palate, open mouth relaxed corners, close nasal, compress tongue. Lighter = distinct twang, raise larynx, lower palate, smile, open nasal, broaden tongue. Exercises start low (women G4/men C4 single notes; scales women D4 / men F3). Warning: support from C5/C4 (women/men) in Curbing. Never pp, never ff. Unintentional distortion in Curbing: hold not right, not metallic enough, too loud, hold + trying full metal, too much hold, palate→larynx drop, too quiet.
- OVERDRIVE: full metal, "bite" (Joker smile, upper lip stretched, lower jaw behind), shout "hey". Never air. Upper limit women D5–Eb5, men C5; no lower limit. Vowels high: EH, OH only (EE/I/A → EH; OO/O/OR/AH → OH). Volume mf–ff (quieter possible low). The higher, the louder required; singing TOO QUIETLY in Overdrive at high pitch harms. Low part: dry/squeezed unless larynx cautiously lowered → dark rich. Colour: easiest lighter/twangier; darker risky. "The lighter you make a metallic mode the better you protect the voice." Warnings: women extra support from F4 up; men C4–C5 caution. Unintentional distortion: lost bite → Neutral; Curbing hold + too loud; palate lowering larynx; too quiet. Don't loosen jaw/open mouth too much in Overdrive (lowers larynx → lose metal).
- EDGE (formerly Belting): full metal, DISTINCT twang required (duck, crying infant, witch, diving plane, siren, "meow"/"niet"), screamy, light, sharp. Never air. All pitches incl. above C6, no lower limit. Vowels high: I, EH, A, OE (EE lures to Neutral; AH→A; OO→OOE, O→OE, OH→OEH). Volume mf minimum, usually louder high. Colour alterable least. Raise larynx, broad tongue on upper molars, smile/corners, lower palate, open nasal (ok if nasal). If Edge hurts → not twanged enough / larynx not high enough → twang more, raise larynx, smile, louder. Don't give notes "room" (lowering larynx/raising palate) → lose metal.

## p123-136 Edge colour, summaries, transitions

- Edge: light is safe; darker only slightly, experienced only. Exercises: G4/C4 start; low E4/C4 down. Edge must not be too quiet (harms). Edge doesn't need excessive energy — "if Edge feels like lots of energy you're supporting a tension that counteracts it; focus on twang". Unintentional distortion in Edge: losing twang, TOO MUCH twang, constriction, Curbing hold w/ Edge volume, too quiet, larynx too low.
- Summary table p129 (pdf130): Neutral: with/without air, loose jaw, all vowels, all pitches, quiet (loud only high), soft. Curbing: no air, HOLD, I/UH/O, all pitch, medium, restrained. Overdrive: no air, BITE, EH/OH, max D5-Eb5 F / C5 M, loud, shout. Edge: no air, TWANG, I/EH/A/OE, all pitch, loud, scream.
- Chart p130: pitch ranges per mode per sex (Neutral all; Curbing all; Overdrive stops at D5/C5 with skull; Edge all).
- PRACTISE-VOWELS: Neutral w/ air = AH (quiet, H attack); Neutral w/o air = OO (classical colour, Curbing attack); Curbing = O (medium, plaintive); Overdrive = EH (loud, shout); Edge = I (loud, scream). Exercise: 5-note scales alternating modes (2 notes Neutral-with-air AH pp/mp, 2 notes Overdrive EH f/ff) from D4 (F) / D3 (M).
- INAUDIBLE TRANSITIONS TABLE (mutual vowels):
  N→C: I, UH, O | N→O: EH, OH (not above D5/Eb5 F, C5 M) | N→E: I, EH, A, OE
  C→N: I, UH, O | C→O: ** assisting mode (via Edge) | C→E: I, A/UH
  O→N: EH, OH (limit) | O→C: ** assisting (via Edge) | O→E: EH (limit)
  E→N: I, EH, A, OE | E→C: I, A/UH | E→O: EH (limit)
  Neutral with air → metallic must go via Neutral w/o air.
  Assisting: Curbing I → Edge I → Edge EH → Overdrive EH. (Curbing O → Overdrive OH needs no assist.)
- Pitch & modes: low part: all modes (Neutral, Overdrive, Edge most common). Middle: all (Neutral, Curbing, Overdrive common). High: all; women Overdrive only to D5–Eb5. Very high: Neutral, Curbing, Edge; no Overdrive.
- Transition pitch sweet spots: women Neutral→Curbing D4–F4. Women Curbing→Neutral/Edge high E5–F5. Curbing→Overdrive women F4–A4 / men E4–G4. Overdrive→Edge women A4–C5 / men G4–Bb4 (start transition early; Edge by D5 women / G4 men).

## p137-155 Using the modes

- Curbing→Edge: women from A#4, twang at ~D5, be in Edge E5–F5; men C4 start, Edge F4–G4. Curbing→Neutral w/o air: women D5 → by E5–F5; men D4 → by G4. "Direct the mode toward Edge/Neutral sooner rather than later."
- SOUND COLOUR × MODE × PITCH (chart p139): Neutral widest range of colours; Curbing & Overdrive allow darker; Edge lighter; higher pitch → only lighter colours (esp women). Dark in high part: women only Neutral; men all 4. Darker metallic = greater risk of breaks/constriction; lighter is safer. Want Edge sound w/o volume → Curbing; even quieter → Metal-like Neutral. Dark Overdrive only to ~Bb4 (F) / G4 (M). Dark colour makes transitions inaudible (classical).
- Choose mode closest to centre for the sound, then colour it, rather than pushing a mode to its margin.
- Classical: female ff middle → Curbing; male ff middle → Overdrive; male high C → Edge.
- VOLUME × MODE × PITCH: more metal → louder possible; higher pitch → louder. Neutral quiet everywhere; loud only from high part. Curbing quiet low, medium middle, powerful high. Overdrive medium/powerful low; louder up; disproportionately loud near limit (C5–D5 F, A4–C5 M). Edge medium/powerful low, louder up.
- Even sound across range requires SEVERAL modes: quiet = Neutral all the way; medium women: Overdrive low → Curbing middle → Neutral high; men: Curbing whole range; powerful women: Overdrive low → Edge (change ~Bb4–B4), men: Overdrive → Edge ~F4 (Edge before Overdrive gets louder than Edge). Common untrained pattern: Overdrive low + Neutral high → break.
- Advanced volume exercises (crescendo through range: Neutral w/ air I → Curbing I → Overdrive EH → Edge EH; decrescendo: Overdrive EH → Curbing UH → Neutral AH → Neutral+air AH; etc.)
- VOWELS & MODES: speech-like vowels in high part only in Neutral with air. Vowel's choice of mode by volume (high part): e.g. women loud: EE→Neutral w/o air, I→Edge/Curbing, EH→Overdrive, OO→Neutral, O→Curbing, OH→Overdrive, A→Edge, AH→Curbing; quiet: all Neutral. Men loud: EE→Curbing/Neutral, I→Edge/Curbing, EH→Overdrive, OO→Curbing/Neutral, O→Curbing, OH→Overdrive, A→Edge, AH→Edge/Curbing.
- DIFFICULTY (high part) table: Neutral with air: all vowels possible (EE,EH... AH some difficult). Neutral w/o air: EE, OO, O, OH, UH?, AH easy; I, EH, A difficult. Curbing: I, O, UH easy; EE, OO, A, OE(?) difficult; EH, OH AVOID. Overdrive: EH, OH only; all others AVOID. Edge: I, EH, A, OE easy; EE difficult; OO, O, OH, AH AVOID.
- ALTERATION MAP (high part): Neutral w/ air: none. Neutral w/o air: EE/I/EH→EE; A/AH→AH; OO/O/OH→OO. Curbing: EE/I→I (EH avoid); A/AH→UH; OO/O→O (OH avoid). Overdrive: EE/I/EH/A→EH (most avoid); OO/O/OH/AH→OH. Edge: EE/I→I; EH→EH; A/AH→A; OO/O/OH→OE.
- Worked example lyric "If you wanna do it, we can work it out" re-spelled per mode (Curbing: "if yO wOn-nO dO it, wI cUHn wUHrk it UHut"; Overdrive "EHf yOH wOHn-nOH dOH EHt…"; Edge "if yOE wAn-nA dOE it, wI cAn wOErk it Aut"). ← great for lyric feature.
- Compressed tongue = operatic vowels in any mode.
- Speech: loud → Overdrive, quiet → Neutral. Darker = authority; too low pitch tires voice.

## p156-179 Speaking, Sound colour, Mic, Effects intro

- Speaking: all modes equally healthy; Neutral only healthy when quiet. Over-articulation triggers constriction.
- Sound colour chapter: acoustic rule — larger tract = darker. Six parameters each with dark/light pole:
  Epiglottic funnel: necessary twang (dark) ↔ distinct twang (light)
  Larynx: lowered ↔ raised (MINOR change within pitch-determined zone; larynx must rise with pitch; high-note trouble = larynx not raised enough)
  Tongue: compressed (operatic "cucchiaio") ↔ broad (on upper molars)
  Mouth: relaxed corners ↔ smile
  Palate: raised ↔ lowered
  Nasal passage: closed (oral) ↔ open (nasal)
  Interactions: raising palate tends to lower larynx; raising larynx tends to lower palate & broaden tongue.
  Singer should be able to choose mode independent of each colour parameter; respect mode limits (no low larynx in Edge; compressed tongue/relaxed mouth/raised palate/closed nasal in Edge needs lots of control).
  Twang + lowered larynx = "carrying/focus", classical; a loud twanged non-metallic low-larynx tone sounds bad close-mic'd.
- Mic: 3 spheres (1 bass boost/proximity, best w/ air; 2 working sphere, adjust distance with volume; 3 acoustic). Unvoiced→voiced consonants to avoid pops (P→B, T→D, K→G, F→V, S→Z, CH→J...).
- EFFECTS INTRO: effects list (distortion, creak/creaking, rattle, growl, grunt, screams, intentional breaks, air, vibrato, ornamentation). Must SOUND spontaneous but be designed to repeat nightly healthily. Healthy effects made in the vocal tract.
  LEVELS: 1 vocal cords (creak, creaking, air, hammer vibrato); 2 false folds (distortion); 3 arytenoids/cuneiform, epiglottis, aryepiglottic folds (arytenoid rattle, saliva rattle, growl); 4 hypopharynx/piriform; 5 soft palate, uvula, back wall, back tongue (uvula rattle, back-tongue rattle); 6 rest (vowels, colour). Grunt, scream, breaks, laryngeal vibrato = combinations.
  Procedure: principles → mode → sound colour → effect. If hoarse, stop. Effects cost extra ENERGY; volume usually DROPS when an effect is added (to keep volume change mode & add energy; distortion sounds different per mode). Effects must be designed for song, singer's energy, temperament, emotion — authenticity.
- DISTORTION: noise+tone; 100% = only noise. Usable in ALL modes, subject to mode limits. Often combined with creaking and rattle. False folds (level 2) vibrate slower → distortion; above true folds so volume can be added. Sensation: imagine twanging a lot; back of tongue pulled back/up; may tickle palate/ears but must NOT tickle vocal cords. Balance noise/note = artistic choice. Keep underlying mode healthy — the mode's correctness protects.

## p180-198 EFFECTS detail

- DISTORTION: adding distortion needs MORE support even though volume DROPS. Twang a lot, small vocal tract → accept LIGHT sound colour at first. Recipe: twang funnel a lot, raise larynx, back of tongue up & back toward high back wall ("mash a pea" — higher = safer), smile wide, LOWER palate. Never raise palate / lower larynx (lose distortion, hurts). Find via Overdrive "bite" + noise in mouth cavity w/o vocal cords; "annoyed" sound; light happy sigh → AH; Neutral flat quiet GEE → add distortion. If tickles/hurts → STOP, more twang, higher larynx. Colour must not get darker while distorting; darker only when expert. Common mixups: getting RATTLE (rolling — back tongue/uvula vibrating) or GROWL (epiglottis tilts back, Kermit) instead of distortion. Amount: less twang = less distortion; SMALL distortion needs MORE control. MODE determines character, colours, volumes, vowels, pitch.
  - Distortion per mode: Neutral w/ air (quiet, gentle, suffering, slow laryngeal vibrato, Bowie/Joplin); Neutral w/o air (howl, quiet–loud); CURBING (most common — distortion with lyrics is mostly Curbing; loud or less); Overdrive (powerful only, angry, isolated words); Edge (powerful, aggressive, screaming+strangled).
  - FULL distortion = 100% noise, no tone/mode; isolated effect.
  - Exercises: noise alone → add to Neutral flat quiet AH; Bb4 (F)/C4 (M); toggle distortion on/off without changing note; make it smaller; 5-note scale same amount; selected notes; avoid back vowels OH/O/OO at first; then phrase; changing modes with distortion = very advanced.
  - Warnings: twang always, larynx always raised, don't raise palate, higher = more support, twang vowels higher (→ I, A), avoid dark colours early.
  - Unintentional distortion causes list (mode not positioned, Curbing too loud, not twanged, larynx low, constriction, lost bite/twang, palate up, metallic modes too quiet).
- CREAK & CREAKING (level 1, true folds; complex but regular vibration): produced by "two slightly opposite ways of using the voice" = slightly incorrect mode use, controlled; LOW volume. CREAK = at beginning/end of phrase, intimacy, about to break down; mic sphere 1; Neutral or Curbing. Found by preparing a metallic mode (Curbing hold) then reducing volume until metal can't be sustained → creak; more volume → clear note; creak can glide into any mode. CREAKING = creak during singing, a delicate version of distortion; Curbing decreasing volume, or Overdrive EH turned down, or Overdrive OH → OR (vowel slightly wrong for mode) with less volume. Involuntary creaking (studio) can become permanent & blur modes; practise without too. Working in marginal area needs extra energy. Higher pitch → more support; don't lower larynx much.
- RATTLE (level 3 or 5): 4 types — arytenoid rattle (L3; twanged Neutral + light easy vibration; if sounds like distortion, place higher, less support, softer), saliva rattle (L3+; gargle saliva added to distortion), uvula rattle (L5; rolling rrr at uvula, snore), back-tongue rattle (L5; kkkrrr/gggrrr hissing). Alone sounds less "mean" than distortion; with distortion makes it stronger.
- GROWL (level 3): epiglottis tilts back almost covering vocal cords (hollow dark "covered" sound) + arytenoids vibrate against epiglottis (rolling). Coarser than distortion; jazz, R&B, gospel (Armstrong). Recipe: raise larynx slightly, pull back of tongue BACK not down ("swallow the tongue"), twang; Kermit "la la la" ("cnoedle"). Clear throat with closed mouth. Darker possible by raising palate carefully. Less twang = more discreet; small growl needs control. All pitches (twang & larynx up more with pitch); acoustic illusion: drop an octave to growl then back (ear doesn't notice). Volume pp–ff. All vowels; beware back vowels. Colour darker than distortion; dark easier low. Warnings: tickle/scratch = lost twang or back tongue lowered (too far down). Mode under growl must stay centred (all 5 modes). Exercise "note-growl-note".
- GRUNT (levels 1–5 combined): whole larynx vibrates in OPEN position; arytenoids, AE folds, epiglottis, base of tract vibrate → very low frequencies, powerful, dark/hollow/booming; can be without tone (cords open). Death/black metal. Mic sphere 1. Find: relaxed cords, exhalation with air + support, add sound not tone; "annoyed/stupid" sound; forcing-with-support; Neutral low dark more air than note; add Overdrive "bite" for volume. Needs LOT of support. Via growl: epiglottis upright, remove arytenoid drumming. Mouth smaller as resistance. Volume p–ff (remove air to get louder). Warnings: tickle = lost support = forcing.

## p199-213 Screams, breaks, air, vibrato

- SCREAMS: just a high-pitched note in a given mode (levels 1–6); light colour, twang. Mode must be held; losing mode = cough/tears. Neutral scream (twanged metal-like Neutral, AH/EE, broad tongue behind molars, up to C6 F/C5 M; slow vibrato → Ian Gillan "Child in Time"). Curbing scream (I/O/UH, raise larynx & palate, half-metal). Edge scream (I/EH/A, ff, very sharp). Multi-mode screams: vowel determines mode (OO Neutral → A Edge; James Brown: Edge high → darker, drop pitch, end Neutral). Screams + distortion (slight → full), + rattle. Flageolet screams (Prince).
- INTENTIONAL VOCAL BREAKS: abrupt change metallic ↔ Neutral (or flageolet); one side ALWAYS Neutral/flageolet. Bigger mode difference = more distinct. Breaks table (mutual vowels): Curbing↔Neutral I,UH,O; Overdrive↔Neutral EH,OH (pitch limit); Edge↔Neutral I,EH,A,OE. Yodel = rapid Overdrive↔Neutral with 6th/7th leaps. Distinct break: far-apart modes, same vowel, exaggerate the FROM mode. Break to silence. Practise inaudible transitions afterwards so breaks don't become involuntary.
- AIR ADDED: level 1. ONLY in Neutral → low volume. Studio tricks (whisper track layered) fake loud breathy. Must first control Neutral without air. Small amount of air suffices; more air = forcing. Extra support needed. Hiss must be smaller than note initially. Distinct twang + light colour magnifies impression of air. Even attempting air in metallic mode can damage.
- VIBRATO: hammer (pulsation, level 1, "line of attacks", sheep/machine gun; Arab, flamenco, Piaf) vs laryngeal (larynx up/down, pitch variation, slower, wider; crooners, jazz, blues). Train speed with metronome (3,4,5 pulses per beat at 60 bpm); align with rhythmic subdivision. Common: start straight, add vibrato toward end; speeding up/slowing down for intensity. Rock rarely starts with vibrato; classical always. In metallic modes vibrato must be added consciously & costs more energy. Too much vibrato/wobble = slack support. Involuntary fast vibrato = lack of support. Image: support 100 / volume 50 → spare 50 = vibrato.

## p213-224 Ornamentation, vocal problems

- Ornamentation: "grid" from hammer vibrato or slowed laryngeal vibrato; subdivisions 2,3,4,6 per beat; change note per beat then per pulse; practise grid in all modes (energy differs per mode). Case: jazz singer only in Curbing (easier for runs) but tried ff → hoarse; use Overdrive for loud, Curbing for runs.
- Hoarseness: if long-lasting → laryngologist/phoniatrician with STROBOSCOPY (or high-speed/videokymography). Causes: muscle tension imbalance (technique, stress, emotion), inflammation (smoke, dryness, infection, reflux, allergy, meds), structural (early nodules, cyst, polyp, haemorrhage), nerve damage (rare). Singing hoarse with bad technique → vicious circle → nodules. Nodules: total voice rest 7–10 days (no whisper, no throat clearing) is Sadolin's first recommendation; speech therapy; surgery. Then fix technique. (NB: some of her claims e.g. "not necessarily unhealthy to sing on swollen cords if done correctly" are her opinion — app must not adopt that; app keeps 'see a laryngologist'.)
- Morning voice, mucus, don't over-clear throat; steaming; alcohol dilates vessels; smoking; sleep; physical strength — fatigue at end of concert → constriction → high notes fail first, volume drops.
- Stick to technique by physical sensation rather than hearing (bad monitoring → oversinging).

## p225-245 Emergency aid, warm-up, hearing, studio/live, improv, interpretation

- Emergency programme: breathing, support rock, sss 1 min; co-ordinate support & sound 15–45 min sessions: soft, quiet, low, breathy sounds driven by support alone, then accentuations, then clearer, then higher; then 5-note EE/OO in easiest mode (Neutral/Curbing), octave; then I/O, EH/OH, A/AH.
- Warm-up = brushing up technique (support, twang, centre of modes, healthy effects); not physiologically required; don't spend the concert's energy. Most strain happens when tired.
- Hearing: inner (bone) vs outer; recordings sound different; earplugs/in-ears → pitch risk; hold technique by physical sensation.
- STUDIO vs LIVE: same song may need different modes. Live needs louder. Methods: "FULL METALLIC" (sing live in Overdrive & Edge only — safest at loud volume) or "ONE STEP UP" (studio Neutral w/ air → live Neutral w/o air; Neutral w/o air → Curbing; Curbing → Overdrive/Edge). Case: folk singer used Curbing from studio on tour → hoarse. Compressors/limiters: don't push louder. Same sound-check phrase every time. Headset mic: no spheres.
- Improvisation & phrasing: start/end lines on other notes; chord-note exercises; pentatonic, blues scales; rhythm exercises with metronome; ahead/behind beat.
- INTERPRETATION method: translate lyrics, moods in lyric vs melody, story, previous history, CHARACTER (never yourself), who/where/why, progression, turning points, SUBTEXT per verse; then choose modes, vibrato types, attacks, pitch, improvisation, volume, effects per the character ("Wendy mostly uses Neutral and Curbing, little vibrato, few ornamentations, effects only when angry"). Song has "mind of its own". Band joint expression.
